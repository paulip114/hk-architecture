# Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance (Cap. 499)

Draft working rules for architects. Source: Cap. 499 Verified Copy, version date 11.4.2025, read on 2026-11-07. No tool in this repo checks these rules, so a tool's PASS says nothing about them.

## How to tell whether a scheme is affected
1. Check the works against **Schedule 2**. Part I lists designated projects by type, in groups A to Q, including reclamation, dredging, sewage, waste, utilities, drainage, community, tourist and recreational projects. Part II lists decommissioning projects.
2. Many items depend on size or on distance from an existing or planned sensitive use, such as a residential area, place of worship, educational institution or health care institution. Read the exact item, including its thresholds.
3. Check these two items in particular:
   - **P.1:** a residential or recreational development within Deep Bay Buffer Zone 1 or 2, other than New Territories exempted houses.
   - **Q.1:** earthworks, dredging and other building works partly or wholly in an existing or gazetted proposed country park or special area, conservation area, existing or gazetted proposed marine park or marine reserve, site of cultural heritage or site of special scientific interest. The item lists exceptions, including certain minor maintenance works and New Territories exempted houses.
4. Ordinary residential or commercial development outside those items is not a designated project merely because of its type or size. Confirm this against the full Schedule, and check any associated works such as drainage, sewage or a substation separately.

## What the Ordinance requires
- **Environmental permit (s.9(1)):** a person must not construct or operate a Part I project, or decommission a Part II project, without a permit or contrary to its conditions.
- **Earlier approvals (s.9(2)-(4)):** the section exempts certain projects that had approvals or had started before the Ordinance took effect, including s16 permission and BA consent. A material change to an exempted project needs a permit. Read the section for the dates and conditions.
- **Route (s.5):** apply to the Director of Environmental Protection with a project profile, advertised in a Chinese and an English daily newspaper. The application is either for an EIA study brief, or for approval to apply directly for a permit where s.5(9), (10) or (11) applies.
- **Report and permit:** the EIA report is approved under s.8, and the permit is applied for under s.10. Schedule 3 lists major projects that require EIA reports. Listing in Schedule 3 does not remove the need for a permit (s.9(5)).
- **Penalties (s.26):** contravening s.9 is an offence. On a first conviction on indictment the penalty is a $2,000,000 fine and 6 months' imprisonment. On a first summary conviction it is a level 6 fine and 6 months' imprisonment. Second and later convictions carry higher penalties.

## Not covered here
The Technical Memorandum (s.16), the timing of each step under s.5 to s.10, permit conditions (Schedule 4), appeals, and the thresholds of individual Schedule 2 items. An environmental consultant should advise on any scheme that may be affected.

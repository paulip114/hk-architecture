# Building Management Ordinance (Cap. 344)

Draft working rules, limited to what affects an architect's design and A&A work. Source: Cap. 344 Verified Copy, version date 13.7.2025, read on 2026-11-07. No tool in this repo checks these rules, so a tool's PASS says nothing about them.

## What matters for design and A&A work
- **Common parts (s.2, Schedule 1):** the whole of a building, except parts specified or designated in a registered instrument for the exclusive use of an owner. Unless so specified or designated, they include the parts listed in Schedule 1, such as external and load-bearing walls, structural supports, roofs, corridors, staircases, shared vents, water tanks, pipes, ducts and lifts.
- **Why it matters:** check the deed of mutual covenant (DMC) and registered instruments before proposing A&A works that touch these parts. Building approval does not give the right to alter them.
- **Designating common parts in new schemes:** APP-152 requires greenery areas to be designated as common parts, and the BA may require the DMC to reflect this (Appendix D, paras 8 and 10). Other GFA concessions may have similar conditions.

## Owners' corporations and decisions (Part II, s.14)
- **Management committee (s.3):** a meeting may be convened by the manager, a person authorised by the DMC, or an owner appointed by owners of at least 5% of the shares. The resolution needs a majority of votes, supported by owners of at least 30% of the shares.
- **Resolutions (s.14):** a corporation may resolve on the control, management and administration of the common parts, or on their renovation, improvement or decoration. The resolution binds the committee and all owners.

## Deeds of mutual covenant (Part VIA)
Schedule 7 provisions are implied into DMCs (s.34E), subject to the exceptions in that section. Read Schedule 7 and the DMC itself when a project depends on them.

## Not covered here
Corporation procedure and meetings, manager and procurement duties, funds and accounts (ss.20-21), dissolution and winding up, Lands Tribunal jurisdiction, the 2024 amendments in detail, and the full text of Schedules 7 and 8. A solicitor should advise on rights under a DMC.
